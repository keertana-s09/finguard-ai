## 2. Anomaly detection (Isolation Forest)
Contamination chosen on validation months: **0.01** (search: 0.005->0.48, 0.01->0.5517, 0.02->0.4762, 0.03->0.4068, 0.05->0.2979).
Test months: 3267 expenses, 41 injected anomalies.

| Method | Precision | Recall | F1 | Flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Isolation Forest (used in app) | 0.812 | 0.634 | 0.712 | 32 |
| Isolation Forest + one-hot category (ablation) | 0.615 | 0.390 | 0.478 | 26 |
| Rule: amount > 3x typical | 0.296 | 0.976 | 0.455 | 135 |

Note: the injected anomalies are defined as 5-12x the typical amount, which is exactly what the ratio rule looks for, so the rule has an unfair advantage in recall on this synthetic data. It also raises many more alerts (lower precision), because natural spending has long tails. Compare F1 and the number of flagged transactions, not recall alone.

## 3. Spending forecast (Ridge Regression)
Target: regular monthly spending (one-off anomalies excluded). Time-based split - train points: 126, test points: 72 (2025-10 .. 2026-09, 6 profiles). Mean actual monthly spending: ₹50,768.

| Method | MAE (₹) | MAPE |
|---|---|---|
| Ridge Regression | 6,539 | 13.3% |
| Historical average baseline | 6,574 | 13.3% |
| 3-month moving average baseline | 6,810 | 14.1% |

Interpretation: monthly spending contains a lot of random variation (a few large purchases decide the total), so even the best model cannot be very precise. Compare Ridge with the baselines above before claiming an improvement.
